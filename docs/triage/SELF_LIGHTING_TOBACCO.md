# Triage — integrated self-lighting tobacco concept

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: **STOP / archive**.

This public note intentionally omits chemistry, materials, dimensions, mechanisms and test instructions.

## Classification

One product family with two conceptual activation variants. It is not split into separate projects.

## Evidence

Published patent literature describes self-lighting cigarettes across multiple generations, including integrated ignition, friction activation, protected caps, separated activation elements and removable modules. The generic concept therefore has very low novelty and uncertain freedom to operate.

Representative public records:

- [US3994305A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3994305A/en)
- [US8215317B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8215317B2/en)
- [WO2012123679A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2012123679A1/en)
- [US20140113239A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140113239A1/en)

European policy also requires reduced ignition propensity for cigarettes and regulates manufacture, ingredients, emissions, packaging and sale:

- [Commission Decision 2008/264/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2008/264/oj/eng)
- [EU tobacco product regulation](https://health.ec.europa.eu/tobacco/product-regulation_en)

## Portfolio decision

Score: **24/100**.

- The convenience benefit is weak because lighters are cheap and ubiquitous.
- Fire, burn, transport, toxicology and product-liability exposure dominate the value.
- Decades of close prior art make broad patent protection unlikely.
- Tobacco advertising and product rules make cheap customer acquisition and launch difficult.
- No functional prototype, demand evidence or commercially defensible architecture exists.

Do not prototype, ignite, formulate, purchase reactive materials, advertise, contact manufacturers or publish enabling detail.

## Revisit gate

Reconsider only if all of the following exist:

1. a distinct, non-obvious safety architecture identified without public disclosure;
2. a professional prior-art and freedom-to-operate opinion;
3. written regulatory and transport classification advice;
4. an insurable path and independent safety/toxicology plan;
5. demand evidence strong enough to justify those costs.

Until then, preserve the provenance and keep the concept archived.
